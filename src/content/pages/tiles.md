Tiles are chosen with the room, not after it. Our designers plan the surfaces alongside the brassware, furniture and lighting, so a slab, a mosaic and a floor read as one scheme rather than three decisions.

## What we carry

The slab and tile library in the showroom is drawn from the European manufacturers we have specified for years:

- **Mirage** — large-format porcelain and architectural slabs from Pavullo, Modena
- **Peronda** — Spanish porcelain and ceramic with a strong line in stone and terracotta looks
- **Emilgroup** — Emilceramica, Ergon, Provenza and Viva: four Sassuolo houses under one roof
- **Novabell** — Italian porcelain with a deep range of wood, stone and concrete effects
- **Azulejos Benadresa** — Castellón porcelain in large and extra-large formats
- **Museum Surfaces** — marble, onyx and stone reproductions in 6 mm and 12 mm slabs
- **Energieker** — Italian porcelain for floors, walls and outdoor continuity
- **Cooperativa Ceramica d'Imola** — one of the oldest ceramic houses in Italy, founded 1874

Samples go home with you; full slabs are on the floor to walk on.

## Choosing well

**Material.** Porcelain for floors, wet rooms and anywhere hard-wearing; ceramic for walls where weight and cost matter more than abrasion.

**Format.** Large formats make small rooms calmer by removing grout lines; smaller formats and mosaics bring texture and grip to shower floors and feature walls.

**Colour and finish.** Matt stone and concrete effects sit well with brushed brass and bronze; polished marble effects with chrome and glass. We will lay the combinations out together in the showroom light.

**Practicality.** Slip resistance for floors, rectified edges for tight joints, and the right adhesive and grout for underfloor heating — specified on the drawing, not left to the tiler.
