This house has one long, top-lit room facing the garden, and the kitchen had to hold its own in it without closing off the view. We kept the furniture to a single timber, walnut, on the tall units, the island and the pantry, and let one material do the talking: a veined porcelain slab that runs up the wall behind the hob and wraps down both ends of the island.

The tall wall takes the working kit. Four Gaggenau ovens sit in a two-by-two block, with a wine column and larder beside them, so the island stays clear for cooking at the flush induction hob and for five stools along the garden side.

Behind the kitchen, a walnut dresser with open shelving handles the everyday crockery and glass, and a separate utility takes the laundry, three machines and a second sink, so none of it ends up in the main room.
